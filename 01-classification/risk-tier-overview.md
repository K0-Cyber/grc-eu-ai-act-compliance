# EU AI Act Risk Tier Overview

## Purpose

This document explains the four risk tiers under the EU AI Act and how they apply to EugAI.

It supports the AI system classification assessment by showing the difference between Unacceptable Risk, High Risk, Limited Risk, and Minimal Risk AI systems.

## Risk Tier Summary

| Risk Tier | Description | Obligations | EugAI Relevance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unacceptable Risk | AI practices that pose unacceptable risks to fundamental rights | Prohibited | EugAI does not perform prohibited practices |
| High Risk | AI systems in defined sectors or regulated products that may affect health, safety, or fundamental rights | Full compliance obligations | EugAI is not High Risk in current deployment |
| Limited Risk | AI systems with transparency obligations, including chatbots and some synthetic content systems | Transparency obligations | EugAI is Limited Risk |
| Minimal Risk | AI systems not falling into the above categories | No mandatory obligations | Not applicable because EugAI is a chatbot |

## Unacceptable Risk

Unacceptable Risk AI systems are prohibited.

Examples include:

- Social scoring by public authorities
- Subliminal manipulation
- Exploitation of vulnerabilities
- Certain biometric identification uses
- Predictive policing based only on profiling
- Certain prohibited biometric or harmful AI practices

## High Risk

High Risk AI systems are subject to full compliance obligations.

Examples include AI used in:

- Employment and worker management
- Education and vocational training
- Credit scoring
- Access to essential services
- Critical infrastructure
- Biometric identification
- Law enforcement
- Migration and asylum
- Administration of justice

High Risk systems may require:

- Risk management system
- Data governance
- Technical documentation
- Record keeping and logging
- Transparency information for deployers
- Human oversight
- Accuracy, robustness, and cybersecurity controls
- Conformity assessment
- EU database registration

## Limited Risk

Limited Risk AI systems have transparency obligations.

Examples include:

- Chatbots
- Virtual assistants
- AI systems interacting with natural persons
- Emotion recognition systems
- Biometric categorisation systems
- Certain AI-generated content or deepfakes

EugAI falls into this category because it is a chatbot interacting with human users.

## Minimal Risk

Minimal Risk AI systems do not have mandatory obligations under the EU AI Act.

Examples may include:

- Spam filters
- AI in video games
- Product recommendation systems
- Non-decision-support productivity tools

Good practice is still to apply transparency, human oversight, monitoring, and responsible AI controls where appropriate.

## EugAI Classification Summary

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Does EugAI perform prohibited AI practices? | No |
| Is EugAI used in an Annex III High Risk context? | No |
| Does EugAI interact with natural persons? | Yes |
| Does Article 50 apply? | Yes |
| Final classification | Limited Risk |

## Key Dates and Compliance Timeline

| Date | Milestone |
|---|---|
| 1 August 2024 | EU AI Act entered into force |
| 2 February 2025 | Prohibited AI practices and AI literacy obligations became applicable |
| 2 August 2025 | GPAI obligations became applicable |
| 2 August 2026 | Article 50 transparency obligations became applicable |
| 2 December 2027 | High Risk obligations for standalone Annex III systems become applicable |
| 2 August 2028 | High Risk obligations for AI embedded in regulated products become applicable |

## Summary

The EU AI Act uses a risk-based structure.

EugAI is not prohibited and is not High Risk in its current customer service use case.

Because it is a chatbot that interacts with natural persons, it is classified as Limited Risk and must meet Article 50 transparency obligations.
